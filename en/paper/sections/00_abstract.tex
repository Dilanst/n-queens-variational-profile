\begin{abstract} \noindent A queen placement has at most one queen in each row, column, and diagonal in either direction. We study the continuous
problem of maximizing the entropy of a density of mass $\rho$ on the unit square subject to these four constraints. For $\rho=1$, the
functional is the one in Simkin's variational problem for $n$ queens. For $0<\rho<1$, the maximizer is unique, and its density at
almost every point is proportional to the product of the remaining capacities of the four lines through that point. The proportionality
constant $\lambda(\rho)$ is the activity. As the mass approaches one, the remaining capacities of the rows and columns tend to zero.
Dividing these capacities by $x=1-\rho$ gives positive profiles that converge uniformly and, together with the remaining diagonal capacities and the
limit of the rescaled activity $\lambda x^2$, determine the maximizer at mass one. These profiles depend analytically on $x$ near zero
and satisfy a uniform spatial Lipschitz bound up to the full board. The activity $\lambda(\rho)$ of the maximizer determines the
function $F(x)=(1-x)/(\lambda(1-x)x^2)$, which extends analytically to both endpoints and satisfies $\int_0^1\log F(x)\,dx=1+s^*(1)$,
where $s^*(1)$ is the optimal entropy at mass one. Moreover, $s^*(1-x)-s^*(1)=2x\log(1/x)+O(x)$. Numerical extrapolation, with no
certified error bound, gives $F(0)\approx0.0809$ and $s^*(1)\approx-1.9440$, consistent with known values of Simkin's constant. All
results concern the continuous problem. \end{abstract} \noindent\small\textbf{Keywords.} $n$-queens problem, variational entropy,
Euler equations, spatial regularity. \quad\textbf{2020 Mathematics Subject Classification.} 49K27, 05A16, 46N10, 94A17.\par\normalsize
